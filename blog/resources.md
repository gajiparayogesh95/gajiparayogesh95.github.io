## Primary Sources

Always start here. Everything downstream is someone else's interpretation.

<div class="resources-grid">
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Filings — Free</div>
    <h3>SEC EDGAR</h3>
    <p>Annual reports (10-K), quarterly filings (10-Q), proxy statements (DEF 14A) and insider transactions (Form 4) for every US-listed company. The full-text search is genuinely good.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Filings — Free</div>
    <h3>Company IR Pages</h3>
    <p>Investor relations sites carry presentations, supplementary data and archived calls that never make it into filings. Capital markets day decks are especially revealing about strategy.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Filings — International</div>
    <h3>SEDAR+ / Companies House</h3>
    <p>The Canadian and UK equivalents of EDGAR. Essential if you look outside the US, and far less picked-over by other investors.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Screening and Data

<div class="resources-grid">
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Screener — Free</div>
    <h3>Finviz</h3>
    <p>Fast quantitative filtering on P/E, P/FCF, margins, debt ratios and insider activity. Best top-of-funnel tool for narrowing a universe to a shortlist.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Data — Paid</div>
    <h3>TIKR / Stratosphere</h3>
    <p>Clean standardised financials going back 10&ndash;20 years, plus transcripts and segment data. The main thing you are paying for is not re-keying numbers from PDFs.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Data — Free</div>
    <h3>Macrotrends</h3>
    <p>Long historical series for revenue, margins and cash flow. Useful for seeing how a business behaved through 2008 and 2020 &mdash; the only stress tests that actually happened.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Data — Free</div>
    <h3>FRED (St. Louis Fed)</h3>
    <p>Authoritative macroeconomic series: rates, inflation, industrial production, credit spreads. Better to check the actual data than to absorb it secondhand through headlines.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Valuation — Free</div>
    <h3>Damodaran Online</h3>
    <p>Aswath Damodaran publishes updated industry cost of capital, margins and multiples every January, free. Invaluable for sanity-checking your own assumptions.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Transcripts</div>
    <h3>Earnings Call Transcripts</h3>
    <p>Reading five years of calls consecutively shows you which promises management kept and which quietly disappeared. Available via Seeking Alpha, Motley Fool, or company IR.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Industry and Competitive Research

<div class="resources-grid">
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Industry</div>
    <h3>IBISWorld / Statista</h3>
    <p>Market sizing, industry structure and concentration data. Useful for the qualitative half of an analysis, though treat the growth forecasts sceptically.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Primary Research</div>
    <h3>Tegus / AlphaSense</h3>
    <p>Expert call transcripts &mdash; former executives, customers and competitors. Expensive, but the closest thing to Fisher's scuttlebutt available at a desk.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="resource-card">
    <div class="resource-type">Reviews</div>
    <h3>G2 / Glassdoor / Trustpilot</h3>
    <p>Underrated. Customer reviews reveal switching costs; employee reviews reveal culture and whether the operational story management tells is true internally.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Letters and Writing Worth Following

- **[Berkshire Hathaway Annual Letters](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html)** — the full archive back to 1977, free. Start with 1983 on goodwill and 1992 on value versus growth.
- **Oaktree Memos (Howard Marks)** — the clearest writing anywhere on cycles, risk and second-level thinking.
- **Markel Annual Letters (Tom Gayner)** — thoughtful on insurance float and long-horizon capital allocation.
- **Ensemble Capital** — consistently high-quality analysis of competitive advantage and business quality.
- **Base Hit Investing (John Huber)** — practical value investing centred on ROIC and reinvestment runways.
- **Damodaran's Musings on Markets** — valuation applied to whatever is currently controversial, always with the spreadsheet attached.
